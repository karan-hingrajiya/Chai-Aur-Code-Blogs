If you want to build websites, there is one language you absolutely cannot avoid: **HTML**.

When I first started, I thought HTML was "programming." Technically, it's not. It's a **Markup Language** (that's what the 'M' stands for in HyperText Markup Language).

Think of a webpage like a human body.

* **HTML** is the **Skeleton**. It gives the page structure (headers, paragraphs, images).
    
* **CSS** is the **Skin and Clothes**. It makes it look good (colors, fonts, layout).
    
* **JavaScript** is the **Muscles**. It makes things move and interact.
    

Today, we are just focusing on the bones.

---

## Part 1: Anatomy of an HTML Tag

HTML is made up of "tags." A tag is like a command you give to the browser to tell it what a piece of text *is*.

Is this text a headline? Is it a paragraph? Is it a button? You use tags to label them.

### The Basic Formula

Most HTML follows a simple sandwich pattern:

1. **The Opening Tag:** Starts the command (e.g., `<p>`).
    
2. **The Content:** The stuff inside (e.g., "Hello World").
    
3. **The Closing Tag:** Ends the command (e.g., `</p>`). Notice the slash `/`.

## Part 2: Tag vs. Element (Don't confuse them!)

This confuses everyone at first.

* **Tag:** Just the code part with the brackets `< >`. (`<p>` is a tag).
    
* **Element:** The whole package—the opening tag, the content inside, AND the closing tag.
    

Think of it like a burger. The "Tag" is just the bun. The "Element" is the whole burger with the patty and cheese inside.

---

## Part 3: The Odd Ones (Self-Closing Tags)

Most tags need to be closed because they wrap around content. But what if there is no content?

Take an image, for example. You don't "wrap" text inside an image. You just place the image.

These are called **Self-Closing** (or Void) elements. They don't have a closing tag.

**Examples:**

* `<img src="cat.jpg" />` (puts an image)
    
* `<br />` (adds a line break)
    
* `<hr />` (adds a horizontal line)
    

---

## Part 4: Block vs. Inline ( The Layout Rules)

This is the most important concept for understanding how websites are laid out. Every HTML element falls into one of two categories: **Block** or **Inline**.

### 1\. Block-Level Elements

These are the "divas" of HTML. They always start on a new line and take up the **full width** of the page (from left to right), pushing everything else down.

* **Examples:** `<h1>`, `<p>`, `<div>`, `<ul>`.
    
* **Visual:** Think of them like stacking cardboard boxes vertically.
    

### 2\. Inline Elements

These are the "polite" ones. They only take up as much space as they need and sit next to each other on the same line. They don't force a new line.

* **Examples:** `<span>`, `<a>` (links), `<b>` (bold).
    
* **Visual:** Think of them like words in a sentence flowing from left to right.
